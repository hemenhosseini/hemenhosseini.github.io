## XUV & free-electron-laser spectroscopy

At Elettra I work on pump–probe experiments with the **FERMI** seeded free-electron laser and
the **CITIUS** high-harmonic-generation source. Extreme-ultraviolet pulses give element- and
orbital-specific access to the dynamics of electrons in solids, and table-top HHG lets us develop
and test experiments before taking them to the FEL. Related work includes simulations of XUV
propagation in nanoscale hollow-core waveguides and transient-grating techniques combined with
diffractive X-ray optics.

</section>

## Ultrafast magnetism in van der Waals materials

Layered antiferromagnets such as **NiPS₃, FePS₃ and CoPS₃** couple spin, lattice and excitonic
degrees of freedom. I use time-resolved **magneto-optic Kerr effect (MOKE)** spectroscopy to
follow how their magnetic order responds to, and recovers from, femtosecond excitation.

</section>

<section class="rcard" id="2d" markdown="1">
<span class="num">03</span>

## Excitons and carriers in 2D semiconductors and perovskites

Transition-metal dichalcogenides (e.g. **MoS₂**) and halide perovskites are promising materials
for optoelectronics and photovoltaics. I study their photophysics with broadband transient
absorption, including MoS₂ coupled to nanostructured, grating-like substrates where lattice
(Rayleigh) resonances reshape the optical response.

</section>

<section class="rcard" id="microscopy" markdown="1">
<span class="num">04</span>

## Wide-field ultrafast microscopy

During my PhD at Politecnico di Milano I implemented **ultrafast transient holographic
microscopy**, which uses off-axis holography to record pump–probe images over large fields of
view, and applied it to plasmonic gold-nanorod arrays.

</section>
